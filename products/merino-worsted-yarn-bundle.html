About this one

Five skeins in a single dye lot is the whole point of buying yarn as a bundle. Merino bought one skein at a time from a shop shelf will very often come from two or three lots, and the colour shift between them shows as a horizontal band across a finished blanket that nothing afterwards will fix.

This is 100 per cent superwash merino, worsted weight, 100 grams and 218 yards a skein — 1,090 yards in the bundle, which will make a lap throw of roughly 40 by 50 inches at a standard worsted gauge, or two generous adult hats and a scarf with yarn left over.

Superwash means the scales on the wool fibre have been treated so the yarn can be machine washed on a wool cycle without felting. It is the sensible choice for anything that will actually be used rather than admired. It also means the yarn has slightly less spring than untreated merino, which is the honest trade-off.

Gauge on the band is 18 stitches to 4 inches on US 8 needles, but check your own tension before you commit five skeins to anything fitted.
