About this one

Bent-handle shears are the ones with the lower blade offset from the handle, so the blade can run flat along a table while your hand stays above it. If you have only ever cut fabric with straight kitchen scissors, this is the single change that makes the most difference to accuracy.

These are 8-inch shears in hot-forged high-carbon steel with a hardened, micro-serrated lower blade that grips slippery cloth instead of pushing it forward. The pivot is a screw rather than a rivet, so it can be tightened when it eventually loosens, and they can be sharpened rather than thrown away.

With them come twelve 300-yard reels of all-purpose polyester thread in a graded palette — black, white, three greys, two navies, two browns, a red, a green and a cream. Polyester is the sensible general thread: it has a little give, so a seam under strain stretches instead of snapping.

Keep fabric shears for fabric. Cutting paper with them dulls the edge in a way that cutting cloth does not, and nobody has ever un-learned that lesson.

  • Care

    • Cut fabric only. Paper and card will dull the blades quickly and permanently.
    • Wipe the blades dry after use. High-carbon steel rusts.
    • Tighten the pivot screw a quarter-turn if the blades start to separate mid-cut.
    • Have them professionally sharpened rather than using a household sharpener, which will ruin the micro-serration.
    • Keep out of reach of children.
