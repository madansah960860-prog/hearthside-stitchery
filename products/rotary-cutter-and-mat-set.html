About this one

Three tools that have to be bought together because none of them works without the other two. You cannot use a rotary cutter without a mat under it or a ruler against it, and this is the combination most quilting patterns assume you own.

The cutter takes a standard 45mm blade — the size every brand sells replacements for — and has a squeeze-to-open safety guard that closes the moment you let go, rather than a slide catch you have to remember. The handle is ambidextrous.

The mat is 24 by 18 inches of three-ply self-healing PVC, printed with a 1-inch grid and 30, 45 and 60 degree lines on one side and plain on the other. Self-healing means the surface closes behind the blade so you do not end up cutting in old grooves.

The ruler is 6 by 24 inches of 3mm cast acrylic with 1/8-inch markings, marked in both directions so left-handed and right-handed cutting both read the right way up.

A rotary blade is genuinely sharp and it does not feel sharp until it has already cut you. Close the guard every single time you set it down, and keep it away from children.

  • Care

    • Store the mat flat. A mat stored leaning against a wall will warp permanently and never sit flat again.
    • Keep the mat out of direct sun and away from radiators; heat buckles PVC.
    • Wipe the mat with cool water and a soft cloth. Never use hot water or solvents on it.
    • Change the blade when it starts skipping threads rather than pressing harder.
    • Close the cutter's guard every time you put it down.
