About this one

A stitch dictionary is not a book of projects. It is a reference you keep next to you to answer one question: what would this look like if I worked it in that stitch instead? Every one of the 120 patterns here has been knitted as a swatch, photographed flat at close to actual size, and charted.

The patterns are grouped by family — knit and purl textures, ribs, cables, lace, slip-stitch and colourwork — and each entry gives the multiple (how many stitches the pattern repeats over), the row repeat, the written instructions, the chart, and a plain note on what the stitch does in practice: whether it curls, whether it pulls in, whether it eats yarn.

That last note is the part most stitch dictionaries leave out and the part that saves you a ruined project. A lace panel that pulls in by 20 per cent will wreck a set-in sleeve if nobody tells you first.

It is 224 pages, casebound with a sewn spine so it opens flat without cracking, on 130gsm matt paper. Charts are at 12 point with a fold-out symbol key at the back that stays visible while the book is open at any page.
