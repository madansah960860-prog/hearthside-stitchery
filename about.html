About Hearthside Stitchery

Three people, sixteen products, and a Tuesday evening class in Lancaster, Pennsylvania.

Who we are

Hearthside Stitch &amp; Supply, Inc. trades as Hearthside Stitchery from a shop unit at 516 Millrace Road in Lancaster, Pennsylvania. There are three of us: two who pick and pack, and one who teaches the Tuesday evening class that the business grew out of.

We are a retailer. Everything here is bought in from mills, importers and publishers. We do not spin yarn, weave fabric or print books, and we are not going to imply a workshop we do not have.

The three questions a product has to pass

Sixteen products is a deliberately short list. Something gets on it only by answering all three of these.

  1. Can the supplier tell us the specification? Exact fibre percentages, grams per ball, yards per ball, band gauge, whether it is superwash. For fabric: fibre, weight in grams per square metre, thread count, cut size. If a supplier will not give us those numbers, we assume there is a reason and we pass.
  2. Can we state the washing rules? Every fibre product on this site carries its actual care instructions, because the single most expensive mistake in this craft is finding out after forty hours of knitting that the yarn felts.
  3. Would we put it in front of the Tuesday class? That group contains complete beginners and people who have been knitting for fifty years, and something that only works for one of those groups does not go on the shelf.

Dye lots, and why we sell bundles

A dye lot is one batch of yarn dyed at one time. Two balls of nominally the same colour from different lots can differ enough that the join shows as a band right across a finished blanket, and no amount of blocking removes it.

Yarn shops used to solve this by letting you buy the whole lot off the shelf in person. Online, the equivalent is selling bundles. Every multi-skein yarn product here is supplied from a single dye lot, and we print the lot number on a card in the bag. We will not make up a bundle from two lots to complete an order; we will tell you it is out of stock instead.

Where orders ship from

Every parcel is packed and posted from Lancaster, Pennsylvania. We ship within the United States only — all fifty states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico — and we ship to PO boxes and to APO, FPO and DPO addresses. Full detail, costs and timings are on the shipping policy page.

Most orders leave within one to two business days. Three take longer: the Nine-Patch Quilt Kit needs two to three because it is checked piece by piece against the cutting list before it goes, and the Rotary Cutter set and Willow Yarn Basket ship in oversize cartons.

What we will not say

- No claims about hands, joints or eyes. Our crochet hooks have a 0.55-inch soft-grip handle with a 0.7-inch thumb rest. That is a measurement. We will not tell you what it does for arthritis, carpal tunnel, hand fatigue or anything else, because a yarn shop is in no position to make a health claim and it would be unlawful to try. - No claims about craft and wellbeing. Knitting is enjoyable. We do not say it reduces stress, lowers blood pressure, helps anxiety or does anything to your mind. Those are health claims. - No "hypoallergenic" and no "organic". Neither word goes on a product here without certification we can produce, and at present we hold none. - No country-of-origin claims. We do not print "Made in USA" on anything, because we cannot document it to the FTC's "all or virtually all" standard. We can tell you where a parcel ships from, which is a different statement entirely. - No reviews or star ratings. None on this site. Manufacturing them would break the FTC rules on consumer reviews and would be beneath the shop. - No urgency. No countdown clocks, no "only two left" counters, no crossed-out prices we never charged. If a dye lot really is nearly gone, the seasonal letter says so and the product page says "out of stock" when it is.

Skill labels

Some products carry a label: Beginner, Confident or Experienced. These describe what the work asks of you and nothing else.

Beginner
Assumes no previous experience. Every step is written out and photographed.
Confident
You have finished at least one project and can follow a written pattern without help.
Experienced
You read charts, understand gauge, and can adjust a pattern to fit.

They are not ratings, they are not a score, and they are not a judgement about anybody. A label in the footer of every page says the same thing.

Accessibility commitment

This site meets the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.1 at Level AA, and we test rather than assume.

Body text is set in Verdana, which has unusually open letterforms, at a size and line height that keep long instructions comfortable to read. There is no light-grey small print anywhere. Text contrast is at least 4.5 to 1 throughout and better than 7 to 1 for body copy. Colour is never the only carrier of meaning — the colour-swatch chips on product cards each carry a written colour name for screen readers, and the skill labels are words, not colours. Every control is a real button or link with a visible focus outline, every tap target is at least 48 pixels tall, and a skip-to-content link is first in the tab order on every page. Every image has meaningful alternative text and explicit dimensions. The layout reflows to 320 pixels without horizontal scrolling and works at 200 per cent zoom.

Nothing moves on its own: no carousels, no pop-ups on load, no infinite scroll.
